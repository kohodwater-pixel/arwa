## Why these three

Camel Step, Elixir Bunn and Brew92 are the three Saudi roasters named together as local roasters that have entered international roasting competitions [F, S4]. ## Category conventions

All points below are [A] or [I] unless marked; they describe the pattern as recalled and inferred, not as observed today.

- **Roaster and café chain in one.** The leaders run their own branded cafés as well as selling beans [F for Elixir Bunn, S3; A for the others]. They compete with their wholesale customers.
- **Proof by sourcing and competitions.** Origin, micro-lot, process and competition entries are the standard evidence of quality [F, S2, S4]. These are table stakes, not differentiators.
- **English-led names and labels.** Two of the three trade under English names [F, from the names]. Arabic tends to be the translation rather than the lead voice [A].
- **International third-wave look.** Minimal layouts, black / kraft / cream, small technical label text (origin, altitude, process, notes) [A].
- **Messages everyone repeats:** specialty grade, carefully sourced, freshly roasted, passion for coffee [A].

## White space

1. **Arabic-first, by design.** A brand whose Arabic wordmark and Arabic voice lead, with English as the equal second, would stand apart from two English-named leaders [I]. This matches the brief (Arabic primary).
2. **The café's partner, not the café's rival.** A roaster with no retail chain of its own, built around weekly roast-to-order supply, can promise independent cafés something the three leaders structurally cannot: "we do not compete with you" [I]. Needs the client to confirm it has no plans for its own cafés (see Decisions in the handoff).
3. **Reliability as the headline, not origin stories.** Roast date, weekly schedule and delivery day as the visible brand promise [I]. Only the client-confirmed fact "roasts to order weekly" may be claimed.
4. **A natural, local symbol that is not the camel or the desert.** The sidr tree (سدرة) is a native tree with deep cultural meaning in Saudi Arabia [A, general knowledge, not sourced today]. It gives a Saudi root without repeating Camel Step's territory.
5. **Warm colour in a monochrome category.** If the black / kraft / cream convention is confirmed on inspection, a warm, distinctive palette is open [A]. Constraint from the brief: avoid green-and-white combinations close to large international chains, which matters here because a tree name pulls towards green.

## What this means for the brand

1. **Position against the structure, not the quality.** Sidra cannot out-pioneer Camel Step or out-source Elixir Bunn in year three. It can be the roaster that works for independent cafés rather than alongside them.
2. **Lead in Arabic.** Arabic wordmark first, Arabic voice first. This is both the brief and the clearest visible difference from the two English-named leaders.
3. **Make "weekly, roasted to order" the proof point.** It is the only verified claim available and it answers the café owner's real question. Do not borrow competition or award language: the client has none.
4. **Use the sidr tree, but not in green and white.** Explore the tree through form, line or an earthy / warm palette. Stay clear of the camel-and-desert heritage look.
5. **Verify visuals before the creative direction is approved.** The art director should open the three competitors' sites, bags and Instagram and correct the visual rows above; they are assumptions.
