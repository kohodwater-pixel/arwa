## Audience 1 — Independent café owners in Riyadh (priority: wholesale, first 20 accounts)

- **Who:** owner-operators or small groups of one to three branches [A]. Defined in the brief [F, brief].
- **Why they buy:** a consistent cup week after week, fresh stock, a roaster who answers the phone [A]. The brief's own words are "a reliable local roaster" [F, brief].
- **What they fear:** inconsistent batches, late deliveries, running out before the weekend, and a supplier that also runs cafés nearby and competes for their customers [A; the last point is I from the competitor audit].
- **Where they are:** WhatsApp for ordering and relationships, Instagram to judge a roaster's standing, visits and cuppings in person [A].
- **Language and tone:** Arabic first, direct and businesslike, peer to peer. Specifics (roast day, delivery day, minimum order) persuade more than adjectives [A].

## Audience 2 — Home brewers, 22 to 40 (online, subscription)

- **Who:** Arabic first, comfortable in English [F, brief]. Young adults are the core of Saudi coffee consumption growth [F, S9]; filter / dripper brewing is common among young adult coffee drinkers in one regional study [F, S11; Al-Ahsa sample, not Riyadh].
- **Why they buy:** better coffee at home, discovery of new origins, the small ritual of brewing, something worth sharing [A].
- **What they fear:** stale beans with no roast date, paying a premium for something ordinary, choosing the wrong coffee for their brewer, feeling talked down to by experts [A].
- **Where they are:** Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat and X for discovery; online shops and delivery apps for purchase [A, not sourced].
- **Language and tone:** clear modern Arabic with a light Gulf warmth; English coffee terms (V60, espresso, flat white) used naturally alongside Arabic. Confident and friendly, not technical for its own sake [A].

## Shared

- Both audiences read a roaster's bag and Instagram grid as the proof of seriousness [A].
- Both accept specialty vocabulary; neither needs specialty explained from zero [I from S4, S9].
- Western numerals (0123), as set in the brief [F, brief].

## What this means for the brand

1. **Two audiences, one promise.** "Fresh, on schedule, every week" works for the café owner (reliability) and the home brewer (freshness). Build the message hierarchy on that rather than writing two separate brands.
2. **Write Arabic as the original, not the translation.** Keep common English coffee terms where people actually use them.
3. **Be specific, not poetic.** Roast dates, delivery days and clear brew guidance answer the main fears of both groups. Avoid unverifiable superlatives.
4. **Design for Instagram and WhatsApp first.** The social post and the landing page will be judged on a phone, in Arabic, right to left.
5. **Validate before scaling.** Recommend five short conversations with Riyadh café owners to test the "partner, not rival" idea; until then it is an assumption.
