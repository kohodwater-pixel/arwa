## Category

Specialty coffee roasting in Saudi Arabia, sold two ways: wholesale to cafés and direct to home brewers online. Home market Riyadh; GCC delivery still TBC.

## Size (treat as rough ranges, not facts to publish)

- Saudi specialty coffee: one estimate of USD 0.95 billion in 2025, rising to USD 2.11 billion by 2033 [F that the estimate exists, S5; the figure itself is a single vendor's and unverified].
- Broader Saudi coffee market: estimates of USD 1.38 billion (2024) and USD 1.82 billion (2025) from different vendors [F, S6]. Café market (all cafés): about USD 6 to 6.6 billion [F, S7].
- Coffee and tea imports: SAR 6.99 billion (USD 1.86 billion) in 2025 [F, S8].
- The vendors disagree by a wide margin. **No reliable single market-size number was found.** No current count of cafés or roasters in Riyadh was found.

## Trends

- Growth is driven by young Saudis and a café culture that moved coffee out of the home over the past decade [F, S9].
- A reported Vision 2030 goal of one coffee shop per 1,000 people [F as reported, S9]: more cafés means more wholesale accounts to win [I].
- Local roasters now enter international roasting competitions [F, S4]; quality at the top is high, so quality alone does not differentiate [I].
- Tastes are widening beyond traditional Saudi coffee to other styles [F, S10]; filter / dripper coffee is common among young adults in one regional study (Al-Ahsa, not Riyadh) [F, S11].

## Regulation and culture

- 2022 was designated the "Year of Saudi Coffee" [F, S12]; the Saudi Coffee Company aims to raise domestic production tenfold by 2030 [F as reported, S12]. Coffee is tied to national identity and hospitality [I].
- Traditional qahwa remains strong alongside specialty; one unnamed 2025 survey claims 89% stay committed to it [unverified, S12; do not quote].
- Food labelling, Arabic-language labelling and SFDA requirements for roasted coffee were **not researched** in this scope [A: Arabic labelling is required; confirm before packaging work].
- Seasonality (Ramadan, Eid gifting, summer iced drinks) is expected to shape demand [A].

## What this means for the brand

1. **Do not print a market-size figure** in the profile or the deck. The numbers conflict and are not verifiable. "A fast-growing category" is the most that can be said.
2. **The wholesale opportunity is real and structural.** More independent cafés are opening, and each needs a roaster. Speak to café owners as a first-class audience, not an afterthought to retail.
3. **Quality is the entry ticket.** The brand must look credible to a specialty buyer, then win on something else: reliability, partnership, Arabic-first character.
4. **Coffee is a national-pride subject.** A locally rooted name and Arabic-first identity fit the cultural moment; the tone should be proud without making claims about Saudi-grown beans the client has not confirmed.
5. **Check labelling rules before any packaging application** is designed as final.
