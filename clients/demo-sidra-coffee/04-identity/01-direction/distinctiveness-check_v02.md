# Distinctiveness check v02 — Sidra Coffee "Weekly Beat" against three competitors

> **SYSTEM TEST — FICTIONAL CLIENT.** The client is invented. The three competitors are real companies.

**Date:** 2026-11-13  **Prepared by:** Art Director  **Version:** v02
**Replaces:** `distinctiveness-check.md` (v01 main body plus the Creative Director's round-3 addendum). That file is kept unchanged as the record; **this file is the current one.**
**Answers:** quality review r1 issue 1; quality review r2 issues N4 and N5.

## Result

**All three competitors are DISTINCT from Sidra's Weekly Beat identity, on the evidence of their home pages.** Ember stays as specified. No change to the mark, palette, device or typefaces is required by this check. One existing requirement is confirmed: the wordmark must be custom-drawn before any real use.

## Countersignature of the Creative Director's addendum (N5)

I opened the three captures myself on 2026-11-13 and **I confirm the addendum's three verdicts and its conclusion that Ember does not need to move.** I correct two of its descriptions, neither of which changes a verdict:

- Camel Step's headline type is described there as "light, rounded". What I see is a heavy, squarish Arabic face set in mid grey: light in colour, not in weight.
- Camel Step's header symbol is described there as "a line-drawn camel mark". I see a small line-drawn symbol in dark teal-blue, about 40 px high in the capture. At that size I cannot say what it depicts. It is an outline pictorial symbol, which is what matters for the comparison.

## 1. Camel Step — DISTINCT

| | What I saw |
|---|---|
| Logo | A symbol alone in the centre of the header: line-drawn, dark teal-blue on white, no wordmark beside it in this view |
| Colours | Dark teal-blue announcement bar and header icons; white and very pale blue-grey grounds; grey headline and a grey button. No red, no cream, no warm ground |
| Typefaces | Small text consistent with IBM Plex Sans Arabic (reported by the script in the first run). The large headline is a different, heavier, squarer Arabic face |
| Language lead | Arabic, right to left |
| Imagery | Cut-out product photographs of espresso machines and a grinder; a large pale arrow graphic |
| Not seen | A wordmark, coffee packaging, anything below the first 900 px. The hero was caught part-way through a slide change, so its headline is cropped |

**Against Sidra:** a line symbol against a heavy wordmark on seven bars; cool teal-blue on white against Ember, Ink and warm Paper; equipment photography against flat colour and type. Nothing in common in mark, colour or device.

**One overlap, already ruled on:** both use IBM Plex Sans Arabic for text. It is a widely used open typeface and is not, by itself, a resemblance. It does mean Sidra's wordmark cannot stay as outlined Plex Bold: the custom-drawn wordmark is required before real use (decision-log ruling d; amendment 01 section 8).

## 2. Elixir Bunn — DISTINCT

| | What I saw |
|---|---|
| Logo | Stacked bilingual wordmark, black on white: thin calligraphic Arabic «إكسير البن» over a high-contrast serif "ELIXIR BUNN" with a small spaced "COFFEE ROASTERS" line. No symbol |
| Colours | Black on white for the brand; a dark green campaign banner; white bags and box with gold ornament and teal patterned inserts; a yellow button; product cards photographed on a copper-brown patterned ground |
| The red `#D92C20` | **Seen in place: it is the «مخفض» sale label, red text on a pale pink pill, on each product card.** It is not a brand colour |
| Typefaces | Changa for interface text; the logo and banner use calligraphic and serif lettering |
| Language lead | Arabic, right to left, with an English switch |
| Imagery | Styled product photography with drapery, marble and ornament |
| Not seen | Anything below the first row of product cards; packaging other than what is in the banner and the card tops |

**Against Sidra:** thin calligraphic and serif lettering against a heavy, low-contrast sans wordmark; no device against the seven bars; black, green and gold with ornament against Ember, Ink, Paper and no ornament. The open colour risk in v01 is closed: Ember is not close to any Elixir Bunn brand colour.

**Watch item, not a conflict:** the product cards sit on a warm copper-brown photographic ground. It is a textured photograph, not a flat field, and browner than Ember. If Sidra ever photographs bags on a warm patterned ground the two would move closer; the direction already forbids that (flat Paper or Crema only).

## Corrections that stand from v01

- "Arabic-first" is not a visible difference from Camel Step or Elixir Bunn; both are Arabic-led. It separates Sidra from Brew92 only, and is no longer claimed as a differentiator (decision log, 2026-11-13).
- Brew92 is blue-led, not "neutral".
- Nothing seen supports the audit's recall of "warm earthy" for Camel Step or "dark minimal, Latin-prominent" for Elixir Bunn.

## Limits — what this check does not cover

- **Home pages only, first 900 px of each.** No inner pages were viewed for Camel Step or Elixir Bunn in the loaded state.
- Packaging: Camel Step's not seen at all; Elixir Bunn's only as it appears in one banner; Brew92's only as small web photographs in the first run.
- Instagram and other social accounts, cafés, signage, cups and print, for all three.
- Any other Saudi roaster, including the five named in the audit but not audited. No search was made for another brand using a row-of-bars device.
- **This is a visual comparison, not a trademark search or a legal clearance.** The preliminary conflict check on the mark (review r1 issue 16) is a separate, still-open item and is not closed by this document.
