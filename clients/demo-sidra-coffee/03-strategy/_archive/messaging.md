## 4. Tone of voice

### English

Plain, specific, short. Confident without superlatives. Sounds like a supplier who knows the trade talking to a peer, and like a good host talking to a home brewer. English carries the same facts as the Arabic, in its own rhythm.

| | Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cafés | We roast every week, to your order. | We deliver best-in-class coffee solutions to the F&B sector. | Corporate filler |
| Cafés | Short courses for your baristas. | Award-winning beans trusted by Riyadh's top cafés. | Invented awards and clients; never |
| Home | Brewing with a V60? Start with this one. | Embark on a sensory journey of exceptional beans. | Decoration without information |
| Home | Roasted this week. | Simply the finest coffee in the Kingdom. | Unprovable superlative |

### Rules for both languages

- Never claim awards, certifications, customer numbers, named clients, team members or sales figures: none are available.
- Do not name or compare with competitors in public copy.
- Do not state a roast day, delivery day, delivery time or minimum order until the client supplies them.
- No market-size figures (research found no reliable one).
- No claim that beans are Saudi-grown; origins have not been supplied.
- The phone number in the brief is a placeholder; do not publish it.
