## 2. Name check: Sidra / سدرة

### 2.1 Pronunciation and spelling

| | Arabic | Latin |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | سدرة (with diacritics: سِدْرة) | Sidra |
| Pronunciation | sid-ra, two syllables, stress on the first | SID-rah |
| Full brand name | سدرة للقهوة (sidra lil-qahwa) | Sidra Coffee |

- Arabic to Latin: سدرة can be written Sidra, Sidrah, Sedra or Sedrah. "Sidra" is the shortest and the one already in the legal name. **Use "Sidra" only**, and expect customers to search "Sedra" as well.
- Latin to Arabic: someone who only sees "Sidra" may write سيدرا or سدرا. The correct form ends in ة and has no ي. The Arabic lockup should always be present in Arabic-facing material so the spelling is learned from the brand, not guessed.
- English speakers: most will say SID-rah. A minority may try "SIGH-dra". Low risk.
- Spoken test: easy in both languages, no sounds that are hard for either audience (no ع، ح، خ، ق in the master name).
- Unvocalised سدرة has no competing reading in common use. Diacritics are not needed in the logo.

All points above: **not verified** (specialist's assessment; native Saudi proofreader to confirm).

### 2.4 What the variety finding means

The name is fixed, so this is a risk to manage, not a reason to reopen it.

1. **In English, "Sidra coffee" already means something.** To a specialty buyer it reads as "coffee of the Sidra variety". Home brewers who know the term may expect that variety in the bag.
2. **Search.** English searches for "Sidra coffee" will return variety listings from roasters worldwide. The brand will be hard to find in English by name alone. Arabic search for سدرة للقهوة is far less crowded (not verified).
3. **Trademark.** A variety name used for coffee may be treated as descriptive in class 30. This is a question for trademark counsel, not something this document can answer.
4. **Product confusion.** If the roastery ever stocks a Sidra-variety lot, the label would read "Sidra Sidra".

**Recommended handling**

- Lead with the Arabic. سدرة للقهوة carries the tree meaning and has no variety confusion for an Arabic reader.
- In English running copy, pair the name with what the company is: "Sidra, a Riyadh roastery" or "Sidra Coffee Roastery", so that "Sidra" reads as the company, not the bean. The value proposition in `messaging.md` already does this.
- Let the visual identity make the tree unmistakable. This is input for the art director, not a decision made here.
- Never name a product line "Sidra Origins", "Sidra Reserve" or anything that sets "Sidra" next to an origin or variety word.
- Raise point 3 with counsel before filing.

**Overall view:** the name is a good fit in Arabic and for the primary audience. It is weaker in English because of the variety collision. Score: Arabic 8/10, English 5/10.

## 3. Product line names

### 3.1 Approach

The personality is plain-spoken and the brand is a branded house, so line names should work as clear labels on a bag and an order form, not as sub-brands competing with "Sidra". Two routes were explored:

- **Route P, plain labels:** the words a café owner already uses when ordering.
- **Route N, single Arabic words:** one short Arabic word per line that can become a name in both scripts.

Longer list considered and dropped: المجلس / Majlis (overused in the category and tips into folkloric); فيّ / Fay (unvocalised it reads as the preposition "في"); الجذر / Al Jidhr (hard in Latin, heavy); المصدر / Al Masdar (already a well-known name in the region in another sector, not verified); الأساس / Al Asas (flat); سدرة الأصل / Sidra Origins (variety collision, see 2.4).

None of the names below has been checked against trademark registers, competitors' product ranges, domains or handles: **not verified** for all nine.

### 3.2 House blend

| # | Arabic | Latin / English | Rationale | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (recommended) | خلطة البيت | House Blend | The term cafés already order by and the one `messaging.md` already uses. Nothing to explain. "البيت" also carries the hospitality value quietly. | Generic, cannot be owned or registered. Acceptable under a branded house. |
| 2 | اليومية | Al Yawmiya / The Daily | The coffee a café pours all day. Short, Arabic-led, usable as a name in both scripts. | "Daily" sits beside a brand built on "weekly" and may blur the rhythm message. |
| 3 | ظلال | Dhilal / Shade | The shade of the sidr tree: a place to sit, hospitality. Ties the line to the master name. | "Dh" is awkward in Latin and will be misspelled. Poetic, and the brand is plain-spoken. |

**Recommendation: خلطة البيت / House Blend.** It is the plainest and it matches how the primary audience buys. If there is more than one blend, distinguish by use, in the same plain register: خلطة البيت للإسبريسو / House Blend, Espresso and خلطة البيت للفلتر / House Blend, Filter. Whether more than one blend exists is **not confirmed by the client**.

### 3.3 Single-origin range

| # | Arabic | Latin / English | Rationale | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (recommended) | محاصيل مفردة | Single Origins | Matches `messaging.md` ("محاصيل مفردة وخلطات البيت"). Each coffee is then named by its origin and farm or station, which is the plain fact buyers want. | Generic. Confirm with a native reader that محاصيل مفردة is the term Riyadh buyers use (some roasters say محصول واحد or أحادية المصدر). |
| 2 | مفردة | Mufrada | One word for "single". Short, Arabic-rooted, works in both scripts, gives the designer one word to set large on the bag. | Needs a descriptor in English the first time it appears. |
| 3 | المحصول | Al Mahsool / The Harvest | Puts the crop and the farmer first; fits "plain facts". | Slightly generic, and "harvest" suggests seasonality the client has not confirmed. |

**Recommendation: محاصيل مفردة / Single Origins**, with each coffee labelled as country, then region or producer, then process. **Rule for this range:** the word "Sidra" must not sit next to an origin or variety word on these labels (see 2.4). The origins the client stocks have not been supplied: **gap**.

### 3.4 Monthly subscription

| # | Arabic | Latin / English | Rationale | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (recommended) | كل شهر | Every Month | Echoes the brand's own rhythm phrase "كل أسبوع / Every week". Says exactly what it is, written natively in both languages, no transliteration needed. Lets copy write: «تُحمَّص كل أسبوع، وتصلك كل شهر.» / "Roasted every week. Delivered every month." | Not ownable and weak as a search term. Always pair with the master name: اشتراك سدرة، كل شهر / Sidra subscription, Every Month. |
| 2 | موعد | Maw'id / On Schedule | Draws on the approved value "على الموعد". Warm: a standing appointment with your coffee. | Promises delivery timing, which the client has not confirmed. The apostrophe makes the Latin form awkward. |
| 3 | الاشتراك الشهري | Monthly Subscription | The pure descriptor. | A function, not a name. Fine as a menu label, flat on a box. |

**Recommendation: كل شهر / Every Month.** It is the only option that extends the approved strategy idea rather than just labelling the product. The sample line above assumes subscription coffee is roasted in the weekly roast, which is **TBC** in `brand-context.md` (roast-to-order for single online orders); do not publish that line until confirmed.

### 3.5 The recommended set, and the alternative

| Line | Recommended (Route P) | Alternative set (Route N) |
|---|---|---|
| House blend | خلطة البيت / House Blend | اليومية / Al Yawmiya |
| Single-origin range | محاصيل مفردة / Single Origins | مفردة / Mufrada |
| Monthly subscription | كل شهر / Every Month | موعد / Maw'id |

Pick one route for all three; do not mix. Route P is recommended because it fits "plain-spoken, not technical for show" and asks nothing of a café owner placing an order. Route N is the choice if the client wants names with more character on the bag; it would need Latin spellings fixed and each word screened.

## 4. Tagline assessment

Source: `03-strategy/messaging.md`, section 1. None of the three has been checked for prior use by another brand: **not verified**.

| # | Line | Strengths | Weaknesses | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | «تُحمَّص لطلبك، كل أسبوع.» / Roasted for your order. Every week. | States the positioning in five words. Built on a verified fact (weekly roast to order for wholesale). Specific, no superlatives, uses approved vocabulary. Arabic and English each read natively. | For home brewers it depends on a fact that is still TBC (roast-to-order for single online orders), so "works for both audiences" in `messaging.md` is not yet safe. The passive needs its diacritics: without them تحمص can be read as "you roast". Very close to the brand promise line, so the two will need distinct jobs. | **Recommended**, with the two conditions below. |
| 2 | «قهوتك على موعدها.» / Your coffee, on schedule. | Warm and idiomatic in Arabic. Draws on the value "على الموعد". Works for both audiences. | Does not mention roasting, so it could belong to any delivery service. Promises timing the client has not confirmed. | Hold. Better as a supporting line for delivery or subscription once timing is confirmed. |
| 3 | «محمصتك، لا منافسك.» / Your roaster, not your rival. | The sharpest and most memorable of the three. Speaks directly to a café owner's real worry. | Rests on the "no cafés of its own" claim, which the decision log (2026-11-13) keeps out of all deliverables. Excludes home brewers. Defines the brand by an implied competitor, against the rule not to compare with competitors in public copy. | Do not use now. Keep on file; revisit only if the client confirms the claim, and then only for wholesale material. |

**Recommendation: option 1, «تُحمَّص لطلبك، كل أسبوع.» / "Roasted for your order. Every week."**

Conditions:

1. Until the client confirms that single online orders are also roasted to order, use it in wholesale-facing material only, or get the confirmation first.
2. Keep the diacritics on تُحمَّص wherever the line is set, and ask the identity designer to check that the chosen Arabic typeface carries the damma and shadda cleanly at small sizes.

This agrees with the brand strategist's recommendation; the audience condition is the one change.
