# Branding rules

## Strategy before visuals

- No logo, palette or typeface is proposed until positioning, audience and personality are written down and approved in `03-strategy/`.
- Every visual decision must trace back to a line in the strategy. If you cannot say which strategic point a choice serves, the choice is decoration; remove it or justify it.

## Decisions are recorded

- Approved decisions go into the client's `brand-context.md` the moment they are approved, with the date.
- Rejected directions are kept in the stage folder under `_archive/` with one line on why they were rejected. Do not re-propose a rejected direction.

## Presenting options

- Present two or three genuinely different directions, never one, never variations of the same idea.
- Each direction has a name, a one-sentence idea, and the strategic reason it exists.
- State a recommendation. The client decides, but the agency has an opinion.

## Identity system minimums

An identity is not complete until it has:

- Logo: primary, secondary/stacked, icon-only; Arabic, Latin and bilingual lockups where the brand is bilingual
- Clear space and minimum size, tested at 16 px and at 10 mm
- Colour: primary, secondary and neutral palettes with HEX, RGB, CMYK and Pantone values; contrast pairs that meet WCAG AA
- Typography: Arabic and Latin families that are licensed for the client's use, with a hierarchy
- Imagery direction, graphic elements, and at least five real applications
- Misuse examples

## Rules made late apply everywhere

When a rule is added or changed after work has started (a name rule, a colour correction, a withdrawn claim), every existing deliverable is checked against it and re-issued if it breaks it, and `brand-context.md` is updated the same day. A rule that lives in one document only is not in force.

## Originality and rights

- No marks, typefaces, photos or illustrations are used without a licence the client can rely on. Record the licence in `04-identity/licences.md`.
- Check a proposed name and mark for obvious conflicts in the client's markets and say plainly that this is not a legal trademark clearance.
- Never imitate an existing brand's identity.
